# Golden task

Two labelled sets of short texts, one in English and one in Russian, that cifarium is checked on:
`cifarium build` runs on the 20 examples of each, and the 100 held-out ones show whether the model it
picked holds on texts the build never saw (`measure.py`). The last run of that measurement is in
[results.md](results.md).

## What is in the two parts, and what is not

| | `english/` | `russian/` |
|---|---|---|
| Source | [Banking77](https://github.com/PolyAI-LDN/task-specific-datasets), PolyAI | [MASSIVE](https://github.com/alexa/massive) 1.1, `ru-RU`, Amazon |
| Licence of the source | CC BY 4.0 | CC BY 4.0 (built on SLURP, also CC BY 4.0) |
| Texts | online banking queries, annotated with intents | voice-assistant commands, translated and localised from English |
| Labels | `new_card`, `card_security`, `transfers`, `top_up` | `будильник`, `календарь`, `почта`, `соцсети` |

**The Russian part is not support requests.** The task asks for real requests to a support service, in
both languages, under a licence that lets the files sit in this repository. For English, Banking77
comes closest: the queries are about a bank's service. Its README says "online banking queries" and
does not say who wrote them, so whether real customers did could not be checked. For Russian, no such set
was found (a search of the Hugging Face hub in November 2026): the Russian translations of Banking77
and of CLINC150 carry no licence and look machine-translated, and the Russian support sets that do carry a
licence, which the search found, are generated by language models or translated from a generated set. MASSIVE is the best that was found with an open licence and
human-made Russian: they are short commands to a voice assistant, not complaints to a shop or a
bank. It tests the multilingual models and the choice of the model on Russian text; it does not say how
cifarium does on Russian support requests. The labels of the two parts are not the same, so the parts
cannot be compared with each other, only each with itself.

## How the labels were chosen

Fixed before any model saw the texts, and not changed after.

- **English.** Four routing topics of a bank's support, each made of five Banking77 intents that belong
  together: `new_card` (order_physical_card, get_physical_card, card_arrival, card_delivery_estimate,
  activate_my_card), `card_security` (lost_or_stolen_card, compromised_card, card_swallowed, pin_blocked,
  change_pin), `transfers` (pending_transfer, failed_transfer, transfer_not_received_by_recipient,
  transfer_timing, cancel_transfer), `top_up` (top_up_failed, top_up_reverted, pending_top_up,
  top_up_limits, verify_top_up). The pairs of labels that sit close are meant: two are about cards, two
  about moving money, so a model that only tells topics apart roughly does not pass by luck.
- **Russian.** Four MASSIVE scenarios that sit close in pairs: `alarm` and `calendar` (both about
  time), `email` and `social` (both about messages). The label names are those in Russian.
- Four labels, 30 texts each: 20 to build from (5 per label) and 100 held out (25 per label). The
  sets are balanced; a skewed set was not tried.

## How the texts were chosen

`prepare.py` does it, and the same sources with the same seed give the same files, byte for byte.

1. The sources are downloaded and checked against pinned SHA-256 sums. Banking77: the training and test
   files together. MASSIVE: the training part of `ru-RU`.
2. MASSIVE only: a text is kept if all three annotators took it for its intent (`intent_score` 1 in
   `judgments`). About 10% of the texts in the four scenarios are dropped, because the source labels
   some of them wrongly. Banking77 has no such mark, so its labels are used as they are, and some are wrong.
3. Spaces are collapsed, text is put in Unicode form NFC. A text that appears twice, or under two intents, is dropped.
4. For every label, 30 texts are drawn at random (seed 2026) and spread evenly over the label's intents:
   six from each of five (English), 10 or 8 or 7 or 15 from each of two to four (Russian).
5. Of the 30, five are drawn for `examples.jsonl`; the other 25 are `heldout.jsonl`. The order in the files is shuffled.

Every line has `source`: the intent the text came from (Banking77) or the intent and the id in MASSIVE.
The build ignores it.

## Files

```
english/  cifarium.yaml  examples.jsonl (20)  heldout.jsonl (100)
russian/  cifarium.yaml  examples.jsonl (20)  heldout.jsonl (100)
prepare.py   builds the two parts from the sources (standard library only)
measure.py   the measurement: scores on the 20 against scores on the held-out part
results.md   what the last run of measure.py printed
```

`cd english && cifarium build` runs the build on the 20 examples. To run it with `license: any`, add that
line to a copy of `cifarium.yaml`.

`python examples/measure.py` counts the vectors of all 120 examples of a part on every model of the catalog
once, keeps them in the cache of cifarium (`golden/`, outside git), and prints the tables; the second run
needs no browser. `--refresh` counts again, `--part english` or `--part russian` takes one part.
